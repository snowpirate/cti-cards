Overview

Hive was a ransomware-as-a-service operation that ran from June 2021 until its infrastructure was seized in January 2023, extorting more than $100 million from over 1,500 victims across healthcare, government, manufacturing, IT, and communications. Its defining characteristic was not technical sophistication — its early encryptor contained a cryptographic flaw that academic researchers publicly broke — but operational aggression against victims for whom downtime is measured in human consequences: it took an Illinois health system offline, forced Costa Rica's national health service to shut down its Single Digital Health Record system, and encrypted India's largest integrated power company.

Hive matters far more as a law-enforcement case study than as a live threat. In late July 2022 the FBI covertly penetrated Hive's own network and stayed inside for roughly seven months, quietly generating decryption keys and handing them to victims — over 300 to organizations mid-attack across 48 states, plus more than 1,000 to earlier victims — preventing an estimated $130 million in ransom payments before the coordinated seizure on 26 January 2023. It is one of the clearest public examples of a deliberate strategic shift: the Bureau chose sustained disruption and victim recovery over waiting to build arrest cases, and announced no arrests on takedown day.

That choice has a visible downside in the record. Because the operators themselves were never in custody, the capability did not die with the infrastructure: in October 2023 a new RaaS brand, Hunters International, appeared using an encryptor with roughly 60% code overlap with Hive's. Hunters denied being Hive, claiming it had simply bought the source code. It ran until announcing its own shutdown on 4 July 2025, whereupon the operation rebranded again as World Leaks — dropping encryption entirely in favour of data-theft extortion, and still claiming victims into 2026. The Hive lineage has therefore outlived the Hive brand by more than three years.

Why this card exists, and an important attribution correction: this profile was opened from an incident report in which an intrusion's initial-access IP — 66.70.179[.]236 — was matched against a public threat feed whose filename attributed it to Hive. That match does not hold up. The FBI's FLASH-20260521-001 (21 May 2026) lists that exact address as an exit node of the "First VPN Service," a criminal VPN active since roughly 2014 with 32 nodes in 27 countries that was rented by at least 25 different ransomware groups. The address is shared anonymization infrastructure, not Hive-owned infrastructure — and the intrusion in question post-dated Hive's seizure by two years. See Section 08 for the full reasoning; it is a useful worked example of how a feed filename becomes a false attribution.

03
Attack Lifecycle Per CISA/FBI/HHS AA22-321A and FBI investigation data

Initial access came predominantly through exposed remote services protected by a single factor. Affiliates logged into RDP, VPN and similar remote-access protocols using valid credentials with no MFA, and where MFA did exist on FortiOS VPN gateways they exploited CVE-2020-12812 — a flaw so trivial it barely deserves the word "exploit": simply changing the case of the username caused the gateway to skip the second factor. Where remote services were not available, they exploited Microsoft Exchange through the ProxyShell chain (CVE-2021-34473, CVE-2021-34523, CVE-2021-31207) and CVE-2021-42321, or sent phishing mail with malicious attachments.

Consolidation and pre-encryption preparation followed a consistent, noisy pattern. Affiliates killed processes belonging to backup software, antivirus and file-copying tools; disabled AV and antispyware through registry modification; and cleared Windows event logs via wevtutil.exe cl system / security / application. Recovery was systematically dismantled before the encryptor ran: vssadmin.exe delete shadows /all /quiet to destroy shadow copies, and bcdedit.exe /set {default} bootstatuspolicy ignoreallfailures to suppress Windows recovery. Helper scripts hive.bat and shadow.bat automated portions of this and then deleted themselves.

Exfiltration preceded encryption, supporting the double-extortion model. Hive affiliates used Rclone synchronising to Mega.nz, with anonymous file-sharing services — AnonFiles, Send.exploit.in, UFile, SendSpace, PrivatLab — as alternates. Stolen data fed HiveLeaks, the TOR publication site used to pressure non-paying victims.

Encryption and extortion then ran across Windows, Linux and ESXi targets using dedicated builds (Windows_x64_encrypt.exe/.dll, Linux_encrypt, Esxi_encrypt). Hive's distinguishing implementation detail was that it wrote a *.key file to the volume root (C:\ or /root/) and dropped HOW_TO_DECRYPT.txt instructing the victim not to modify or delete it, because that key file was required for decryption — a design that made the key file itself a high-value forensic artifact, and one of the hooks the Kookmin University researchers pulled on when they demonstrated recovery without paying.

Victims were funnelled to a TOR sales portal with live chat for negotiation. Notably, the FBI later assessed that only 20–25% of Hive victims reported their victimisation to law enforcement at all — which is why the Bureau had to push decryption keys out through field offices and 72 legal attachés rather than simply notifying a known victim list.

06
Tools, Malware & Infrastructure
Hive Encryptor (Golang, Jun 2021 – Feb 2022) Ransomware · Custom
The original cross-platform encryptor. Wrote a *.key file to the volume root required for decryption and dropped HOW_TO_DECRYPT.txt. Its key-management scheme contained a cryptographic weakness that Kookmin University researchers published a practical recovery method against in February 2022 — the flaw that ultimately forced a full crypto redesign.
Hive Encryptor (Rust, Mar 2022 – Jan 2023) Ransomware · Custom
Rewritten in Rust following BlackCat/ALPHV's lead. Rust's concurrency primitives enabled faster parallel encryption, while string encryption — strings held in the .rdata section and XOR-decrypted with constants at runtime — significantly raised analysis cost. Shipped with a rebuilt cryptographic scheme specifically to close the academic decryption method.
Esxi_encrypt / Linux_encrypt Ransomware · Hypervisor-targeting
Dedicated builds for VMware ESXi and Linux, the ESXi variant first observed in Golang around October 2021 and later ported to Rust. Encrypting the hypervisor rather than individual guests collapses an entire virtualised estate in one action and sidesteps in-guest EDR entirely.
hive.bat / shadow.bat Script · Custom
Helper batch scripts automating cleanup and recovery destruction, notable for deleting themselves after execution — a deliberate, low-effort anti-forensic step that removes the clearest narrative artifact of the pre-encryption sequence.
Rclone + Mega.nz Exfiltration · Legitimate/Dual-Use
The exfiltration backbone. Rclone is a legitimate, widely deployed cloud-sync utility, so its presence is not inherently suspicious — detection depends on context (unexpected host, unusual destination, volume) rather than on the binary itself. Alternates included AnonFiles, Send.exploit.in, UFile, SendSpace and PrivatLab.
Native Windows Binaries (LOLBins) LOLBin · Built-in
vssadmin.exe and wmic for shadow-copy destruction, bcdedit.exe to suppress recovery, wevtutil.exe to clear event logs. Entirely signed, entirely native — the destructive phase generated almost no novel binary artifacts for signature-based tooling to catch.
HiveLeaks & TOR Negotiation Portal Infrastructure · Extortion
A .onion sales and negotiation portal with live chat, paired with the HiveLeaks publication site used to pressure non-payers. Both were seized in the January 2023 operation and replaced with a law-enforcement seizure notice.
Rented Anonymization Infrastructure Infrastructure · Third-Party
Like most RaaS operations, Hive affiliates fronted their activity through commercial criminal VPN and bulletproof-hosting services rather than dedicated infrastructure. This is precisely what makes single-IP attribution to Hive unreliable — see Sections 07 and 08 on the First VPN Service exit node that triggered this profile.

The IP that prompted this profile does not attribute to Hive, and the reasoning matters more than the conclusion. The chain ran: an intrusion's initial-access address was checked against a public threat feed; the feed file was named hive_ransomware.txt; the address was therefore reported as tied to a ransomware actor. But FBI FLASH-20260521-001 lists 66.70.179[.]236 as an exit node of the First VPN Service — commercial criminal anonymization infrastructure operating since roughly 2014 across 32 nodes in 27 countries, marketed on Exploit[.]in and XSS[.]is, and used by at least 25 distinct ransomware groups before a French- and Dutch-led takedown. Three independent problems compound: the address is shared, so a hit identifies the rented VPN and not the tenant; the intrusion in question is dated January 2025, two years after Hive's infrastructure was seized and the brand ceased; and the FBI's own advisory cautions that such addresses are historically observed assignments that may later be reallocated to benign services. A feed filename is a categorisation convenience, not an attribution claim — and where shared infrastructure is involved, it cannot be one.

Competing hypothesis on successor continuity. Hunters International's denial — that it bought Hive's source code, website and legacy builds rather than being Hive — is not implausible on its face; source-code sales are a real feature of the RaaS economy, and code overlap alone would be consistent with either story. What tips the assessment toward genuine continuity is Group-IB's reporting that some individuals were approached by Hunters administrators from the same accounts previously used by Hive, plus other operators in the ecosystem continuing to refer to the new RaaS as Hive. Purchased code does not come with the seller's account credentials and social graph. This remains [MEDIUM-HIGH] rather than [HIGH] because the account-reuse evidence rests on a single vendor's reporting; independent corroboration from a second source with visibility into those forums would close the gap.

The strategic lesson is about what a takedown does and does not buy. The FBI's operation was a genuine success on its own terms — roughly $130M in payments prevented, 1,300+ victims given keys, and a demonstrated model for prioritising victim recovery over arrest-building. But no operator was in custody on takedown day, and the capability reconstituted within nine months under a new brand, then rebranded again, and is still extorting organisations in 2026 under a third name with the encryption component discarded entirely. Infrastructure seizure degrades a criminal enterprise; only arrests and asset seizure appear to end one. That evolution — Hive's double extortion → Hunters' double extortion → World Leaks' extortion-only — also tracks a broader ecosystem shift in which encryption is increasingly treated as optional overhead while data theft does the coercive work. For defenders, that means controls tuned to detect encryption events are aimed at the part of the kill chain the lineage has abandoned; exfiltration detection is now where the leverage sits.

09
Defensive Recommendations
01
Enforce phishing-resistant MFA on every remote-access path — VPN, RDP, webmail, and any admin portal. Single-factor remote services were Hive's dominant entry route, and are the control failure most directly implicated in the incident that opened this profile.
Counters: T1133
02
Verify that MFA on VPN gateways actually cannot be bypassed, rather than assuming its presence is sufficient. CVE-2020-12812 let Hive skip FortiOS MFA by changing the case of a username — test authentication edge cases against your own gateways, including case variance and alternate username formats.
Counters: T1190, T1133
03
Alert on recovery-destruction commands as a high-severity, near-certain-malice signal. vssadmin delete shadows, bcdedit /set bootstatuspolicy ignoreallfailures and wevtutil cl have almost no legitimate use on production endpoints and sit immediately before encryption — this is the highest-value detection window in the entire chain.
Counters: T1490, T1070
04
Maintain offline, encrypted, immutable backups covering the full infrastructure — and test restoration. Hive systematically killed backup processes and destroyed shadow copies before encrypting; only backups outside the blast radius survive that sequence.
Counters: T1490, T1486, T1562.001
05
Treat the ESXi management plane as tier-0 and monitor it accordingly. Hypervisor encryptors bypass in-guest EDR entirely; restrict hypervisor management access, enforce MFA on it, and ship ESXi logs off-host where guest-level tooling cannot see the attack at all.
Counters: T1486 (Esxi_encrypt)
06
Baseline and alert on Rclone and cloud-sync traffic by context, not by binary reputation. Rclone is legitimate software; what is anomalous is a server with no business need synchronising bulk data to Mega.nz or an anonymous file-sharing host. This control has grown more important as the lineage moved to extortion-only.
Counters: T1537
07
Do not treat a threat-feed hit on a shared VPN or hosting IP as actor attribution. Corroborate any single-IP match against the feed's own provenance and at least one independent source before naming a group in an incident report — the scenario that produced this card is the cautionary example.
Counters: Analytic error, not a technique — see Section 08
08
Report ransomware incidents to law enforcement even when you do not intend to pay. Only 20–25% of Hive victims reported, which is the direct reason the FBI could not simply notify known victims while holding decryption keys for them. Reporting is what makes key-recovery operations reach you.
Counters: Intelligence gap — see Section 08

OPSEC Procedures Observed infrastructure hygiene, rotation patterns, anti-forensics
Anti-Forensics [HIGH]
Windows event logs were cleared via wevtutil.exe cl against the system, security and application channels, and the hive.bat / shadow.bat helper scripts deleted themselves after execution. The intent is narrative destruction rather than evasion — by the time logs are cleared the encryption is imminent, so the value is in denying responders the reconstruction, not in avoiding detection.
Living-off-the-Land Ratio [HIGH]
The destructive phase leaned almost entirely on signed native binaries — vssadmin, wmic, bcdedit, wevtutil — with custom code reserved for the encryptor itself and exfiltration handled by legitimate third-party software (Rclone). Hive's custom-tooling footprint was therefore concentrated in a single, late-stage artifact, leaving most of the intrusion visible only as behaviour.
Tooling Hygiene [HIGH]
The March 2022 Rust rewrite is the clearest deliberate hygiene investment: string encryption with strings held in the .rdata section and XOR-decrypted at runtime, combined with Rust's optimised output, materially raised reverse-engineering cost. The concurrent cryptographic redesign was a direct, datable response to Kookmin University's published decryption method — Hive treated a public academic paper as an operational incident and re-engineered within days.
Infrastructure Rotation [MEDIUM]
Affiliates fronted operations through rented criminal VPN services and anonymous file-sharing platforms rather than dedicated, attributable infrastructure — the structural reason single-IP attribution to Hive is unreliable (Section 08). Note the limit of this assessment: Hive's own rotation discipline was evidently insufficient against a determined adversary, since the FBI held covert access to its core network for seven months without detection.
Persona & Brand Management [MEDIUM-HIGH]
The post-takedown rebrand sequence is itself an OPSEC pattern: relaunch under a new name with a modified encryptor (Hunters International), then retire that brand publicly — complete with free decryptors, which buys goodwill and muddies continuity claims — while the replacement (World Leaks) has already been running for six months. Group-IB's observation that Hunters administrators contacted people from Hive-era accounts shows the brand hygiene was considerably better than the account hygiene.

Log Destruction via Native Binary T1070.001

Event logs were cleared using the signed, built-in Windows event utility rather than any custom tool — nothing novel lands on disk, and the action is indistinguishable at the binary level from legitimate administration.
wevtutil.exe cl system wevtutil.exe cl security wevtutil.exe cl application (per CISA/FBI/HHS AA22-321A)
Recovery Suppression Chain T1490
EDR BYPASS vssadmin / bcdedit / wmic
Shadow copies were destroyed and Windows recovery suppressed immediately before encryption, using only native utilities. This sequence is the most reliable high-fidelity detection opportunity Hive offered — it is loud, but it happens minutes before impact.
vssadmin.exe delete shadows /all /quiet bcdedit.exe /set {default} bootstatuspolicy ignoreallfailures (per CISA/FBI/HHS AA22-321A; wmic also observed in the same role)
Binary Analysis Resistance — Rust String Encryption T1027
EDR BYPASS Rust encryptor
The March 2022 Rust encryptor held its strings encrypted in the .rdata section, decrypting them at runtime by XORing with constants — defeating static string-based signatures and slowing manual reverse engineering, per Microsoft's analysis of the Rust variant.
Documented mechanism: strings resident in .rdata, decrypted at runtime via XOR with constants (Microsoft Security Blog, 2022-07-05). Specific XOR constants and per-sample key material vary by build and are not published as a stable indicator.
Hypervisor-Layer Execution T1486
EDR BYPASS Esxi_encrypt
Encrypting at the ESXi layer sidesteps guest-resident EDR entirely — there is no agent on the hypervisor to alert, and every guest is impacted without any of them being touched individually. Strictly speaking this is an architectural bypass rather than an evasion technique, which is what makes it hard to instrument against.
Esxi_encrypt build observed from ~October 2021 (Golang), later ported to Rust. No published per-command execution sequence for the ESXi variant as of 2026-09-18.
