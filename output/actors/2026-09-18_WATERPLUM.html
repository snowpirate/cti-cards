Overview

WaterPlum is North Korea's dual-track scheme for turning the global remote-hiring market into a state revenue stream: one arm poses as recruiters and tricks software developers and IT freelancers into running credential-and-cryptocurrency-stealing malware disguised as a job-interview coding test, while a closely related arm places actual North Korean nationals into real remote IT jobs using stolen or fabricated identities. Both tracks are assessed by a seven-nation joint government advisory published today (2026-09-18) to operate under the same chain of command — the 313 General Bureau of North Korea's Munitions Industry Department — meaning the proceeds are not ordinary cybercrime skimmed for personal gain but a funding line that the advisory and prior OFAC guidance tie directly to the regime's weapons programs.

The name "WaterPlum" is new to public reporting as of today, coined by Japanese security firm NTT, but the underlying activity is not: industry researchers have independently tracked overlapping infrastructure, malware, and social-engineering tradecraft since at least late 2022 under at least ten other names — most commonly "Contagious Interview," alongside DeceptiveDevelopment, DEV#POPPER, Famous Chollima, PurpleBravo, CL-STA-0240, Gwisin Gang, Tenacious Pungsan, TAG-121, UNC5342, and Void Dokkaebi. That volume of independent convergence across competing vendors is itself a strong corroborating signal, even before governments spoke on the record.

WaterPlum matters acutely right now for three compounding reasons. First, scale: the joint advisory's own numbers — 30,000+ infected devices, 100+ countries, over $10.71M moved to North Korea in an eight-month window — represent one of the larger publicly quantified DPRK cybercrime waves to date. Second, novelty of response: this is the first time Japan has identified, investigated, and dismantled a domestic North Korean "laptop farm," and the first joint advisory to use the "WaterPlum" designation, signaling governments are treating this as an escalating, coordinated response priority rather than routine cybercrime. Third, the dual-use structure itself is the hard problem: a single successful infection can fund weapons proliferation directly (stolen crypto) and indirectly (a fraudulently-obtained salaried job funneled to the regime), and the two tracks reportedly share infrastructure — the same IP addresses were observed operating laptop farms, cloud-sourcing services, and job applications to at least one Japanese cryptocurrency exchange.

Geopolitically, WaterPlum sits inside a well-established DPRK sanctions-evasion pattern — the same 313 General Bureau lineage and the same "remote IT worker" mechanism that OFAC has been issuing guidance on since May 2022 and that the US Department of Justice has been indicting facilitators for since at least January 2025 — but it is the first activity cluster to receive a coordinated multi-government public attribution naming a single unifying designation across the malware-deployment and fraudulent-employment sides of the operation simultaneously.

03
Attack Lifecycle Two coordinated tracks sharing infrastructure and objectives

Track 1 — Malware-via-fake-interview. WaterPlum actors build recruiter personas — sometimes using AI face-swapping software during live video calls — impersonating AI, cryptocurrency, or NFT companies on social media, job boards, gig platforms, and freelance marketplaces. Targets are drawn into a technical interview or coding assignment and instructed to clone and run a project from GitHub, GitLab, or Bitbucket, or to open a VS Code workspace, to "verify technical abilities" or "fix a bug." The project embeds a malicious npm package (BeaverTail, StoatWaffle) or a VS Code .vscode/tasks.json auto-run configuration (StoatWaffle) that executes the moment the folder is opened and trusted. The initial JavaScript loader downloads a portable Python environment and drops InvisibleFerret for deeper reconnaissance and remote command execution, while OtterCookie/OtterCandy provide RAT functionality, VM-awareness checks, clipboard monitoring, keylogging, and browser/wallet credential harvesting. Stolen data — wallet seed phrases and private keys, browser-stored credentials, clipboard contents, screenshots, and any files matching credential or ID-document patterns — is exfiltrated over HTTP(S) to attacker-controlled C2 infrastructure, sometimes staged via legitimate services like Vercel-hosted pages or URL shorteners to blend with normal developer traffic.

Track 2 — Fraudulent employment infiltration. In parallel, actual North Korean IT workers — based in North Korea, China, or Russia, with smaller numbers in Africa and Southeast Asia — apply for real remote developer/engineer roles using forged identities and resumes, often padded with implausibly broad skill claims. "Enablers" (witting or coerced third-country nationals, including inside Japan and the US) host "laptop farms": residences where employer-issued or personally-purchased laptops sit physically, remotely operated over VPN by the DPRK worker, so the employer's device-location telemetry shows a domestic address. Enablers also stand up and manage VPS infrastructure and receive salary payments into local bank accounts before laundering funds abroad. Successful hires give North Korea a steady, sanctions-evading salary stream and — per the advisory — footholds inside employer networks that can be leveraged for espionage, IP theft, extortion (one documented case: an IT worker extorted a company and leaked its proprietary source code after being paid), or outright sabotage (one case: a hired "maintenance" worker defaced and took down the client's website).

The two tracks are not hermetically separate: the joint advisory confirms WaterPlum actors and North Korean IT workers were observed using the same IP addresses across laptop-farm access, cloud-sourcing platform use, and a job application to a Japanese cryptocurrency exchange — meaning a victim who was merely infected by malware and a company that unknowingly hired a DPRK operative may be facing the same underlying infrastructure and, ultimately, the same chain of command.

06
Tools, Malware & Infrastructure
BeaverTail Stealer / Loader · MITRE S1246
Obfuscated JavaScript malware distributed as a malicious NPM package hosted on GitHub or Bitbucket. Functions as both an initial credential/browser-data stealer and a loader — downloads a customized portable Python environment and deploys InvisibleFerret as the second stage. The original and still most-cited component of this cluster's toolset.
InvisibleFerret Backdoor · MITRE S1245
Python-based backdoor deployed by BeaverTail as a follow-on payload. Provides remote command execution and extended system reconnaissance, and maintains persistent access after the initial JavaScript-stage foothold. A "FlexibleFerret" Go/Python variant adds encrypted HTTP(S)/TCP C2 with dynamic plugin loading and Windows RUN-key persistence.
OtterCookie RAT / Infostealer
JavaScript-based RAT and infostealer, observed since September 2024, evolved through at least a v4 revision. Adds VM-environment detection (qemu/virtualbox/vmware checks) and targeted Chrome/MetaMask credential theft; uses npm packages (node-global-key-listener, screenshot-desktop) for clipboard monitoring, keylogging, and screenshot capture, with heavy obfuscation via encoded index lookups in recent variants.
OtterCandy RAT / Infostealer, Hybrid
Combines OtterCookie's data-theft functionality with a component tracked as "RATatouille," broadening the malware family's post-compromise capability set. Documented in the September 2026 joint government advisory as part of the current active toolset.
StoatWaffle Modular Loader + RAT · npm / VS Code
Modular Node.js malware family combining a loader, credential-harvesting components, and a RAT for persistence and lateral pivoting. Distributed via blockchain-themed VS Code project repositories embedding a malicious .vscode/tasks.json configuration that auto-executes the moment a victim opens and "trusts" the folder — abusing a legitimate developer-tooling trust prompt rather than a software vulnerability.
HexEval Loader / XORIndex Loader npm Package Loaders · MITRE S1249 / S1248
Obfuscated JavaScript-based npm package loaders documented in MITRE's official G1052 group profile — HexEval uses hexadecimal encoding and XORIndex uses XOR-based obfuscation to evade static analysis of malicious npm packages before handing off to the primary payload chain.
"Laptop Farms" & Enabler-Managed VPS Physical + Cloud Infrastructure
Residential locations — typically an enabler's home — housing employer-issued or purchased laptops physically present in-country while a North Korean operator controls them remotely over VPN, defeating device-location checks. Enablers separately stand up and manage VPS infrastructure on the operators' behalf, obscuring true physical location during contracted IT work. Japan dismantled its first identified domestic instance in the lead-up to this advisory.
AI Face-Swapping Software Social-Engineering Tooling
Used during live video job interviews to sustain a fabricated identity under visual scrutiny; the joint advisory notes operators would disable video a few minutes into a call, citing "network issues," consistent with the deepfake tooling's real-time limitations under sustained use.

WaterPlum's most dangerous property is architectural rather than technical: neither track requires defeating a security control. Track 1 asks a victim to run code they were specifically told to run, inside tooling (npm, VS Code) whose entire design point is running other people's code; Track 2 asks a hiring manager to trust a resume and a video call. No patch fixes either problem — only process controls (sandboxing untrusted code, identity-verification rigor in hiring) do, which is precisely why this campaign has scaled to 30,000+ devices despite years of public reporting on the underlying cluster.

Competing hypothesis worth flagging explicitly: public reporting on North Korean cyber activity frequently and loosely groups "Lazarus Group" with Contagious Interview/WaterPlum-type IT-worker-fraud clusters, but the chain-of-command claims differ — Lazarus Group is most consistently attributed to the Reconnaissance General Bureau (RGB), while today's joint advisory specifically names the 313 General Bureau of the Munitions Industry Department for WaterPlum. These may be sibling programs under North Korea's broader cyber apparatus rather than the same organization, and this profile treats them as ecosystem-adjacent, not identical, pending a source that reconciles the two chains of command directly.

Forward risk trajectory is upward in the near term: the joint advisory itself frames this as an early, evolving response ("actors continuously evolve and refine their methods"), Japan's laptop-farm takedown is described as a first, not a pattern, and the underlying economic incentive (regime revenue, including WMD funding per OFAC) gives North Korea no reason to abandon a scheme this profitable absent sustained multilateral disruption. What would most increase attribution confidence on the "unified operator set" question specifically: a leaked or seized WaterPlum operational document showing tasking that crosses both tracks, comparable to what previous Lazarus-adjacent leaks have occasionally provided for other DPRK clusters.

Defensive Recommendations
01
Never execute unvetted code from a job interview, coding assignment, or "verify your skills" request outside an isolated sandbox or disposable VM. Treat this as a standing policy for any recruitment process, not just ones that already feel suspicious.
Counters: T1566.002, T1204.002
02
Open unfamiliar VS Code projects only in Restricted Mode (answer "No" to the trust prompt), which prevents .vscode/tasks.json auto-execution on folder open — the specific mechanism StoatWaffle abuses.
Counters: T1204.002, T1547.001
03
Alert on scripted-download strings — curl, base64, -enc, mshta, Invoke-WebRequest -uri, iwr -uri, or hidden-window flags — appearing in developer-tool child processes.
Counters: T1059.007, execution-stage detection generally
04
Keep cryptocurrency wallet keys and seed phrases off any machine used for freelance/interview code execution, and treat any device that ran an untrusted assignment as compromised — create a fresh wallet on a separate device and move funds rather than trusting in-place remediation.
Counters: T1555.003, T1657
05
Harden remote-hiring verification: cross-check claimed employment history and certifications independently, ask unscripted personal-context questions (hometown, local weather, hobbies) during interviews, and treat frequent camera drops, background voices, or a skills/language mismatch as escalation triggers, not technical hiccups.
Counters: T1199, T1036.005, T1656
06
Never host a remote-work laptop for a third party at your residence without independently verifying the employer and role — this is precisely the "laptop farm" recruitment vector used against unwitting or complicit domestic enablers.
Counters: Enabler/infrastructure-provisioning stage generally
07
Enforce least-privilege access for any newly onboarded remote contractor or subcontractor, and be prepared to revoke access immediately and audit for source-code exfiltration or web-facing changes if DPRK-worker indicators surface post-hire.
Counters: T1199, post-compromise extortion/sabotage cases documented in Section 03
08
Deploy EDR with behavioral (not just signature) detection on any endpoint used for evaluating external code submissions, since this cluster's initial-stage tooling is JavaScript/Node.js-based and evolves faster than static hash-based detection can track.
Counters: T1497, general evasion of static detection

OPSEC Procedures Observed infrastructure hygiene, rotation patterns, anti-forensics
Infrastructure Rotation [MEDIUM]
Enablers independently stand up and manage VPS infrastructure on operators' behalf specifically to obscure the operators' true physical location during contracted work — a persistent outsourced-infrastructure model rather than operators directly rotating their own hosting.
Persona Management [HIGH]
Uses AI face-swapping software during live video interviews to sustain a fabricated identity, but limits exposure by disabling video a few minutes into calls citing "network issues" — an observed, specific pattern the joint advisory calls out directly, consistent with real-time deepfake tooling degrading under sustained scrutiny. Forged resumes deliberately overstate breadth of skill (10+ programming languages, blockchain, and cloud platforms each) to pass initial screening.
Timing & Operational Patterns [HIGH]
Operators observed pausing malicious activity on North Korean national holidays to play games and watch soccer instead — a direct behavioral tell that the operators' actual work calendar follows the DPRK domestic calendar rather than the victim's time zone or the cover story's claimed location.
Tooling Hygiene [MEDIUM]
Consistent use of free machine-translation and AI-assistance service plans, and practiced Japanese pronunciation via text-to-speech software, to compensate for language gaps between the operators' native language and the persona's claimed nationality/location during interviews.
Anti-Forensics / Detection Avoidance [HIGH]
OtterCookie v4+ performs virtualization/sandbox detection (qemu, virtualbox, vmware artifact checks) before executing its data-theft payload, specifically to avoid detonating inside malware-analysis environments. Heavy JavaScript obfuscation via encoded index lookups in recent variants is intended to defeat static/signature detection rather than dynamic analysis.
