## 6. Analyst Notes
LAPSUS$ (MITRE ATT&CK G1004; Microsoft DEV-0537/Strawberry Tempest; Mandiant UNC3661; CrowdStrike Slippy Spider) emerged in mid-to-late 2021 as a financially motivated, extortion-focused group notable for its unusually public operating style — publicizing attacks and soliciting insider access via social media — and for achieving significant impact against well-defended organizations using largely unsophisticated techniques (credential purchasing, SIM swapping, help-desk social engineering, and MFA fatigue) rather than custom malware or novel exploits.

The August 2023 DHS Cyber Safety Review Board (CSRB) report, commissioned in response to LAPSUS$'s high-profile 2022 intrusions (Microsoft, Okta, Uber, Nvidia, T-Mobile, and others), explicitly assessed the group as **not** nation-state directed or sponsored, and attributed core operations to a small number of individuals — several of them minors — based in the United Kingdom and Brazil. This assessment is corroborated by subsequent UK (City of London Police, March 2022) and Brazilian (Polícia Federal, October 2022) arrests. Per standing orders, no nation-state attribution is asserted here, and origin/attribution above [MEDIUM] for anything beyond "UK/Brazil-based individuals" is avoided given the loosely organized, non-state nature of the group.

Two notable technical characteristics: (1) LAPSUS$ has used stolen or abused code-signing certificates (including a compromised Nvidia certificate) to sign malicious tooling, aiding defense evasion — this is flagged as a sourcing gap above because the exact ATT&CK technique (T1553.002, Subvert Trust Controls: Code Signing) was referenced in secondary/academic analysis but not independently corroborated in the primary CISA/Microsoft/MITRE sources reviewed for this card, so it is omitted from the formal TTP table pending confirmation; (2) reporting since 2023 indicates individuals continue to invoke the "Lapsus$" name for unrelated activity, which may represent brand reuse rather than continuity of the original group — this is noted under Section 1 "Last Observed" at [LOW] confidence.

**Gaps requiring human follow-up:**
1. No network/file-based IOCs were available in the sources reviewed — recommend pulling incident-specific IOCs from individual victim post-incident reports (Okta, Uber, Nvidia, Microsoft).
2. Defense Evasion and Exfiltration tactic rows lack a confirmed, source-backed technique ID and are flagged [LOW]/gap rather than populated with an inferred technique.
3. The T1553.002 (code-signing abuse via compromised Nvidia certificate) claim should be verified against a primary source before inclusion in a final/reviewed version of this card.
